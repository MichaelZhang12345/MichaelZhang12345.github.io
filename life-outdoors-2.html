Life Outdoors – 2

I love how different life outdoors can be. One weekend might mean a camping festival with hundreds of people; another might be a few friends cooking around a fire, a family weekend, or simply some quiet time on my own.

I enjoy all of it—the crowds and the quiet, the sharing and the solitude. Outdoors, I can build something, try an idea, sit by the fire with friends, or simply lie in a hammock and do absolutely nothing. That freedom is what I like most.

Life outdoors

The Outdoors, My Way.

I like the slower, more hands-on side of outdoor life. Give me a piece of wood and enough time, and I’ll probably start carving it into a spoon or a bowl. I enjoy cooking over a fire, trying different camping techniques, and swapping ideas with other campers.

Camping festivals are especially fun because everyone brings something different. At one of them, I tried blacksmithing for the first time. I enjoyed it enough that I later found a blacksmith and arranged to forge two camping knives of my own this winter.

Over time, I started documenting the things I try, the lessons I learn, and my outdoor experiences on my camping blog.

We build things, cook outdoors, and turn simple afternoons into little adventures.

We’ve done far more together than we ever remembered to film. Here are a few moments we happened to capture along the way.

COOKED INSIDE BAMBOO

Bamboo rice always starts with a trip into the hills to find fresh bamboo. We cut it, stuff it with rice, sausage, corn, and carrots, then put it over the fire and wait…and wait.

My sister usually starts asking when it’ll be ready long before it actually is. Then we finally crack open the bamboo, the smoky smell comes out, and all that food disappears in minutes.

Hours to make, minutes to eat. Totally worth it.

FROM TOO MUCH GEAR TO A SECRET BASE

The Cabin

I wanted a secret base of my own. I also had way too much outdoor gear, and every camping trip was starting to feel like a house move. So I thought: why not build a cabin and leave everything there?

“Just build a cabin” turned out to be the complicated part. I had to keep the rain out, stop strong winds from turning it into a giant kite, and anchor the structure firmly into the ground. And with a budget limited to my pocket money, every piece of wood had to earn its place.

Eventually, it became my little base in the woods—and a place my friends wanted to come hang out too.

Power for the cabin

DIY Solar Power System

FROM DIAGRAM TO REALITY

When I built the cabin, it was out in the middle of an undeveloped field—no electricity, no facilities, just grass and open space. But once I started spending more time there, I wanted lights, a fan, and a way to charge my devices. Running a power line all the way out there wasn’t realistic. Sunshine, on the other hand, was easy to find.

So I decided to make my own power. I bought a solar panel, charge controller, batteries, and an inverter, figured out how everything needed to work together, and wired the system myself. After some testing, the cabin had electricity of its own.

Keeping food cool

DIY Mini-Fridge

ANOTHER PROBLEM: FOOD.

Once I could spend the night at the cabin, I ran into a new problem: food. Anything fresh I brought with me was fine for the first day, but by the next day, not so much. If I wanted to stay longer, I needed a fridge.

Buying a regular fridge for a little cabin in the middle of nowhere didn’t make much sense. Besides, I already had electricity from my solar system—so why not try building one myself? I got a thermoelectric cooling module, heat sinks, fans, and an insulated box, then figured out how to put everything together and keep the hot side hot and the cold side cold.

It worked. Not perfectly—but well enough to make me start wondering how I could make it colder.
