A SCIENCE-FICTION STORY

Icarus

伊卡洛斯

16K+ CHINESE CHARACTERS

Icarus has spent his entire life beneath a drifting city.

People there work in terrible conditions and rarely live past forty—yet they are perfectly happy.

At sixteen, everyone undergoes a “baptism.” Icarus eventually discovers what it really is: an implant that makes work pleasurable and disobedience painful.

Before his own baptism, he leaves the world he has always known.

For the first time in his life, he sees the sun.

THE FIRST ANSWER

At first, the answer seems obvious.

People are being controlled.

↓

They should be free.

Icarus learns that the happiness of the people below is engineered. Their “baptism” is not a religious ritual, but a system of neurological control: work brings pleasure; disobedience brings pain.

To Icarus, freedom seems like the answer.

THE REVOLUTION

The Piano

A resistance movement asks Icarus to perform before the Queen. He believes the concert will help expose the system and free his family.

Standing before the piano, he imagines his parents smiling freely, his younger siblings growing up outside the system, and a life in which he can finally learn, play music, and become someone of his own.

He presses the first key.

“所有的光都聚焦在了舞台中央的伊卡洛斯……成为了最耀眼的太阳。”

“开始吧。”

“伊卡洛斯按下了琴键，钢琴的盖子突然弹开，一枚导弹从其中飞出。”

The resistance had turned his performance into an attack—without telling him.

THE QUESTIONS

Then the answers get harder.

01HAPPINESS

If happiness is artificially created, is it less real?

Zeus, the artificial intelligence that helps govern the city, argues that engineered pleasure is still biologically real. What makes one kind of happiness seem more valuable than another, he says, is the meaning people attach to it.

“意义不重要吗？”“Doesn’t meaning matter?”
02FREEDOM

If someone is happy inside a system they did not choose, who has the right to wake them up?

Some people are controlled by implants. Others voluntarily spend their lives inside simulated worlds. The line between captivity and choice becomes less obvious.

03A GOOD LIFE

Twenty years of perfect happiness—or eighty years of struggle?

“我……我选择八十年的苦苦索求。”“I… I choose eighty years of struggle.”
04SURVIVAL

If an inhuman system is keeping everyone alive, should it still be destroyed?

Beneath the city, Icarus discovers that its survival depends on an extreme closed-loop biological system. Resources are so scarce that even organic matter from the dead is recycled.

Zeus tells him that without it, the population would starve within a year.

Then Icarus gets what he wanted.

The people are freed.

The revolution succeeds. The implants are disabled.

But the people below do not celebrate.

Having spent their lives dependent on engineered happiness, they turn against the people who freed them. Icarus learns that his own parents may no longer recognize him.

Freedom was the answer.
Until it wasn’t.

“这是一个死局。”

“It is a dead end.”

“逐日的伊卡洛斯最后还是被烧尽了羽翼。”

“In the end, Icarus, who chased the sun, still burned away his wings.”
